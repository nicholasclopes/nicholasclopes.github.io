Supercritical Fluids

My work with supercritical fluids (SCFs) focused on understanding and improving how we model heat transfer mechanisms associated with variable property flows. We used CFD to look at a range of factors that can affect SCF cooling, including thermal boundary conditions, inlet conditions, flow development, channel geometry, buoyancy, and surface roughness. We employed a range of modeling approaches, including correlation-based methods, 2D and 3D CFD simulations, and machine-learning models, to predict and better understand SCF heat transfer behavior. These projects have helped us better understand both the underlying physics of SCF heat transfer and some of the challenges involved in modeling these flow accurately while balancing engineering practicality.

A3 Refrigerant Safety

As part of a team with Oak Ridge National Laboratory, we developed CFD models to study how refrigerants released from HVAC equipment disperse through mechanical rooms. We first used carbon dioxide to develop and validate a unique modeling approach against experimental measurements, in which we employed a two-stage method that captured the high-mach leak before mapping the resulting flow and species profiles into a full-room simulation. We then applied this approach to propane, an A3 refrigerant, to examine how a leak could spread through a mechanical room and where concentrations could become relevant to lower-flammability-limit (LFL) and ignition considerations. This work brought together compressible flow, species transport, transient CFD, and experimental validation to better understand refrigerant safety and potential mitigation strategies for HVAC systems.
