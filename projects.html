Supercritical Fluids

Our work with supercritical carbon dioxide (sCO₂) has focused on understanding and improving how we model heat transfer in these unusual flow conditions. We’ve used CFD to look at a range of factors that can affect sCO₂ cooling, including thermal boundary conditions, inlet conditions, flow development, channel geometry, buoyancy, and surface roughness. Along the way, we’ve compared CFD results with existing heat-transfer correlations and explored artificial neural networks as a way to predict sCO₂ heat-transfer behavior. More recent work has looked at non-circular channels and different flow orientations to better understand how geometry and buoyancy influence cooling performance. Together, these projects have helped us better understand both the underlying physics of sCO₂ heat transfer and some of the challenges involved in modeling it accurately.
